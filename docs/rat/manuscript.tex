\begin{abstract}
We document the fourth pillar of TUBA --- a transcranial focused
ultrasound (FUS) simulation pipeline for the adult rat, built on the
DigiMorph TMM M-2272 dry-skull microCT (29.6\,$\mu$m isotropic,
\emph{Rattus norvegicus}, UT Austin) registered to the Waxholm Space
(WHS) Sprague--Dawley rat brain atlas v4.01 (Kleven \emph{et al.}\
2023, 39\,$\mu$m $T_2^*$-weighted MRI template with 222 labelled
structures). The pipeline shares TUBA's core primitives with the
mouse and macaque pillars; the rat case turns out to be a near-clone
of the macaque recipe rather than the mouse recipe, because the rat
skull's storage convention (slice=AP, row=DV, col=LR) matches the
AMNH macaque and the multiple anatomical openings (orbits ${\sim}6$\,mm,
foramen magnum ${\sim}3$\,mm) defeat the mouse-style pure-hysteresis
cavity extractor. We port the macaque bone-shell cavity
extractor (refactored into
\texttt{tuba.core.cavity.bone\_shell\_cavity} in this work) with two
rat-specific extensions: median-smoothed per-row / per-column plug
boundaries (eliminating stepping at the 118\,$\mu$m voxel pitch),
and a virtual concave-bowl bone dam at the foramen magnum (sealing
the caudal end with a curved surface matching the actual occipital
bone). ANTs Affine then maps the WHS brain mask into rat space, and
the warped cavity is bone-clipped against \texttt{bone | dam}, plus
a 1.5\,mm opening + caudal pad + largest-CC for hygiene. The
production cavity is 2.39\,mL, in the published 1.8--2.4\,mL adult
rat range. PCA pre-alignment is skipped: the
DigiMorph storage is already axis-aligned after a row/col swap, and
the dry-rat eigenvalue ordering AP\,$>$\,DV\,$>$\,LR (no mandible)
breaks the AP\,$>$\,LR\,$>$\,DV assumption baked into
\texttt{find\_pca\_rotation}. Code: \texttt{src/tuba/species/rat.py}
+ \texttt{tuba.\allowbreak atlases.\allowbreak whs}; data manifest +
fetchers at \texttt{src/tuba/data/}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source data}
\label{sec:source}

The skull volume is a microCT scan of an adult \emph{Rattus norvegicus}
cranium, museum specimen \texttt{TMM M-2272}, deposited in the Digital
Morphology Library at the University of Texas at
Austin~\cite{digimorph-rat}. The reconstruction parameters are
summarised in Table~\ref{tab:scan-params}. The scan reconstructs at
29.606\,$\mu$m isotropic voxels in a $1024 \times 1024$ in-plane grid
over 1571 slices, yielding a ${\sim}28 \times 28 \times 46$\,mm
physical extent that contains the full cranium (no mandible
articulated, no soft tissue). The unpacked uint16 stack occupies
${\sim}3.1$\,GB; the compressed Box.com archive is ${\sim}0.6$\,GB.
Strain is unspecified --- TMM M-2272 is a museum specimen, not a
lab-bred Sprague--Dawley.

\paragraph{Comparison with the published target frequency wavelength.}
Rat tFUS in the literature is typically performed at 500\,kHz to
1.5\,MHz; we anchor the design to 500\,kHz to align with the macaque
pillar~\cite{tuba-macaque} and human pillar~\cite{tuba-human} bowl
geometry. At 500\,kHz the cortical-bone wavelength is
$\lambda_{\mathrm{bone}} = 5.8$\,mm ($c = 2900$\,m/s) and the
parenchymal wavelength is $\lambda_{\mathrm{brain}} = 3.08$\,mm
($c = 1540$\,m/s). The native 29.6\,$\mu$m voxel pitch gives
$\sim$196 voxels per cortical-bone wavelength --- substantially
finer than the macaque 60.6\,$\mu$m pitch ($\sim$96\,vox/$\lambda$)
and finer still than the mouse 6.13\,$\mu$m pitch at 15\,MHz
($\sim$28\,vox/$\lambda$). After block-max downsampling to a
237\,$\mu$m ANTs cache (factor 8), the working pitch still gives
$\sim$24 vox/$\lambda$ in cortical bone, comparable to the macaque
242\,$\mu$m cache.

\paragraph{Strain mismatch note.}
The DigiMorph specimen is \emph{Rattus norvegicus} but the museum
record does not pin a strain. The WHS atlas (\S\ref{sec:whs}) is
built from an 80-day-old male Sprague--Dawley rat. We expect a
modest skull-shape mismatch between the museum cranium and the
SD-derived atlas, on the same order of magnitude as the AMNH
museum macaque vs.\ NMT v2 lab-cohort gap in the macaque
pillar~\cite{tuba-macaque}. The Affine warp
(\S\ref{sec:cavity}, \S\ref{sec:registration}) accommodates this
geometrically; the acoustic-property mapping
(\S\ref{sec:acoustic-mapping}) is species-agnostic and unaffected.

\section{Waxholm Space reference atlas}
\label{sec:whs}

The brain anatomy is provided by the Waxholm Space (WHS) Sprague--Dawley
rat brain atlas v4.01~\cite{whs-v4}, an open-access volumetric atlas
acquired ex vivo on a 9.4\,T MR scanner at Duke CIVM from an 80-day-old
male Sprague--Dawley rat~\cite{whs-papp-2014}. We download the v4 pack
zip (which bundles the $T_2^*$ template, v4 label volume, ITK-SNAP
\texttt{.label} table, and Mouse BIRN Atlasing Toolkit packages) plus
the v4.01 label-only patch (Kleven \emph{et al.}\
2023~\cite{whs-v4}) via \texttt{tuba.data.fetch\_whs}. The pack zip
ships at 39\,$\mu$m isotropic and contains:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
  \item \texttt{WHS\_SD\_rat\_T2star\_v1.01.nii.gz} --- the 39\,$\mu$m
        ex vivo $T_2^*$-weighted MR template (the ex vivo MR reference used as the binary brain-mask source
        (label-derived; see \S\ref{sec:cavity}));
  \item \texttt{WHS\_SD\_rat\_atlas\_v4.nii.gz} --- the v4 label
        volume (220 structures);
  \item \texttt{WHS\_SD\_rat\_atlas\_v4.label} --- ITK-SNAP colormap
        + name table (id $\to$ name $\to$ RGB$\alpha$);
  \item additional MBAT / PMOD packages not used by TUBA.
\end{itemize}
The v4.01 patch supersedes the v4 label volume with 222 structures
(112 new, 57 revised), released 2023-04-20 after the
Kleven \emph{et al.}~\cite{whs-v4} publication. Both the v4 and v4.01
label volumes are registered to the same v1.01 $T_2^*$ template;
\texttt{tuba.atlases.whs.\allowbreak WaxholmSpace.\allowbreak
annotation\_path} defaults to v4.01.

An atlas-overview figure (WHS $T_2^*$ template + v4.01 label
volume, three orthoslices through the brain-mask centroid) appears
as Fig.~\ref{fig:whs-atlas} in \S\ref{sec:registration} alongside
the rat-space-warped equivalents, so the native-frame and
rat-space versions can be compared side by side.

\paragraph{Target structures.}
The WHS v4.01 label table includes 222 anatomical structures across
the cerebrum, cerebellum, hippocampal formation, basal ganglia, and
thalamic / midbrain nuclei. The transcranial-FUS rat literature
targets corpus callosum~\cite{rat-cc-tFUS} (analogous to the mouse
pillar~\cite{tuba-mouse}), striatum / caudate
putamen~\cite{rat-stim-strialum}, and hippocampal
formation~\cite{rat-stim-hpc} most frequently. The placement helper
(\S\ref{sec:placement}) accepts the canonical WHS structure name
(e.g.\ \texttt{'Caudate putamen'}, \texttt{'Hippocampal formation'},
\texttt{'Corpus callosum and associated subcortical white matter'})
via the \texttt{.label} parser and resolves it to the warped
annotation centroid at run time.

\section{Orientation determination}
\label{sec:orientation}

The DigiMorph TIFF stack carries no DICOM affine; the only spatial
metadata is the per-voxel pitch (29.606\,$\mu$m isotropic) printed on
the specimen page. We determine the mapping from storage axes
$(s, r, c)$ to anatomical $(R\text{-}L, A\text{-}P, D\text{-}V)$ in
three steps mirroring the macaque recipe (manuscript~\cite{tuba-macaque}
\S Orientation): a per-slice bone-mass profile, a log-binned intensity
histogram, and direct rendering of four anchor slices distributed
through the cranium.

\paragraph{Slice axis (rostro-caudal).}
The bone-mass profile (Fig.~\ref{fig:bone-profile}) is single-domed,
unlike the macaque's bimodal cranium+mandible profile -- the DigiMorph
TMM M-2272 specimen is a dry cranium with no articulated mandible.
The total bone-bearing slice extent is 45.0\,mm (storage slices
36--1556 at 29.606\,$\mu$m), matching the
rat cranio-occipital adult range of 40--50\,mm. The direction is
disambiguated by the four-anchor render
(Fig.~\ref{fig:axial-anchors}): storage slice 112 (5\,\% through the
cranium) displays a thin oval bone ring with empty interior
characteristic of the anterior nasal cavity; storage slice 1480
(95\,\% through) shows the rounded oval of the caudal cranial fossa
near the foramen magnum. The slice axis is therefore rostral at
storage 0 and caudal at storage 1571, and \texttt{SLICE\_FLIP=False}
in \texttt{tuba.species.rat} so that, after the row/col swap and the
closing transpose, storage slice 0 maps directly to $+y_{\max}$
(anterior in RAS).

\paragraph{In-plane axes.}
Both in-plane axes are 1024 voxels (30\,mm at 29.6\,$\mu$m). The rat
cranium has roughly equal L-R and D-V extents in this dry specimen
(${\sim}22$\,mm L-R between zygomatic arches, ${\sim}20$\,mm D-V from
basicranium plate to vault), so the two cannot be told apart by
extent alone. We disambiguate from anchor slice 568
(Fig.~\ref{fig:axial-anchors}): the dental arcade / maxillary palate
forms an inverted-U at the BOTTOM of the image, which is the
ventral side. Storage row 0 is therefore ventral and row 1023 is
dorsal. The storage convention is
$(\mathrm{slice}{=}AP, \mathrm{row}{=}DV, \mathrm{col}{=}LR)$ -- the
\emph{macaque} convention, opposite of the mouse Maga convention
$(\mathrm{slice}{=}AP, \mathrm{row}{=}LR, \mathrm{col}{=}DV)$. We swap
row and col once at load time (\texttt{swap\_row\_col=True}, added to
\texttt{tuba.core.frame.\allowbreak apply\_axis\_flips} in this work)
so the downstream \texttt{SLICE\_FLIP / ROW\_FLIP / COL\_FLIP}
semantics and the closing \texttt{transpose(1,0,2)} match Maga exactly.
After the swap+transpose the volume is in clean RAS world frame
(Fig.~\ref{fig:world-orthoslices}); no further per-axis flips are
needed.

\paragraph{Intensity thresholds.}
The DigiMorph distribution provides 8-bit uint8 TIFFs (subdirectory
\texttt{8bitTIFF/}); the histogram is uncalibrated and intensities
are not Hounsfield. The aggregated intensity histogram across every
16th slice (Fig.~\ref{fig:histogram}) resolves three populations: air
at $\le 31$ ($\sim$93\,\% of voxels), a long soft-tissue / trabecular
plateau in $[50, 180]$ ($\sim$5\,\%), and a cortical-bone tail above
180 with maximum near 245 ($\sim$2\,\%). A single threshold cleanly
separates bone from background:
\texttt{BONE\_LOW}\,$=50$ in \texttt{tuba.species.rat} sits at the
air/soft-tissue separator. \texttt{ALIGN\_PCA\_THRESH}\,$=120$ above
the soft-tissue plateau focuses the PCA cloud on dense cortical bone
(see \S\ref{sec:align}), and \texttt{BONE\_HIGH}\,$=140$ is the
cavity-exclusion ceiling. The slab ramp endpoints
(\S\ref{sec:acoustic-mapping}) are $I_{\mathrm{low}}=50$,
$I_{\mathrm{high}}=170$ -- anchored on the same histogram features.

\section{Downsampling and pre-alignment}
\label{sec:align}

The native 29.6\,$\mu$m volume occupies ${\sim}1.6$\,GB as uint8; we
reuse the streaming block-max downsampler
(\texttt{tuba.core.downsample.\allowbreak downsample\_tiff\_stack\_to\_nifti})
that backs the mouse and macaque pipelines. Two caches are
materialised:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
  \item a 237\,$\mu$m ANTs cache (factor-8 block-max), shape
        $128 \times 196 \times 128$ uint8 (2.3\,MB gzipped);
  \item a 118\,$\mu$m intermediate cache (factor-4 block-max), shape
        $256 \times 392 \times 256$ uint8 (18.5\,MB gzipped). Used
        for cavity extraction and the slab loader.
\end{itemize}

The 118\,$\mu$m working cache is 3.0$\times$ coarser than the WHS
atlas grid (39\,$\mu$m). ANTs resamples in physical world coordinates
so the source-grid mismatch is handled internally; the macaque
pipeline runs at an analogous 3\,\% match (242 vs 250\,$\mu$m) and
the mouse pipeline at an exact 1$\times$ match (25\,$\mu$m on both
sides). The 118\,$\mu$m choice is dictated by cortical-bone
thickness in rat ($\sim$200--400\,$\mu$m) -- finer caches give
overly-thin bone-shell rasters that the cavity extractor cannot
close; coarser caches (e.g.\ the 237\,$\mu$m ANTs cache) put bone at
$\sim$1\,voxel wall thickness and fail to seal foramina entirely.

\paragraph{PCA pre-alignment is skipped.}
The 237\,$\mu$m cache after the row/col swap and the closing
transpose is already in clean RAS frame (Fig.~\ref{fig:world-orthoslices}),
so no further rotation is needed and the Euler angles in
\texttt{tuba.species.rat} are all $0^\circ$. We retain the option to
run \texttt{tuba.core.\allowbreak align.\allowbreak find\_pca\_rotation}
for QC, but the result for TMM M-2272 is misleading: the function
assumes the dry-skull eigenvalue order
AP\,$>$\,LR\,$>$\,DV and labels the middle eigenvector as the L-R
axis. With \texttt{ALIGN\_PCA\_THRESH}\,$=120$ (cortical bone only,
no mandible), the rat actually has AP\,$>$\,DV\,$>$\,LR -- the
absent mandible removes mass from the ventral extent but not the
lateral, while the wide-spread basicranium and dorsal vault dominate
DV variance over the relatively tight zygomatic L-R extent. The
function correspondingly produces a spurious 85$^\circ$ rotation
about the world $y$ axis. We document this as a calibration trap
rather than fix it: visual QC of the orthoslices is faster and more
reliable here, and the rotation needed is small (eigenvector tilts
$<5^\circ$ per axis) enough that ANTs Affine absorbs it.

\section{Endocranial cavity extraction}
\label{sec:cavity}

We initially hoped to reuse the mouse pipeline's pure-hysteresis
cavity extractor (bone\_low closing + caudal-foramen seal +
connected-component selection)~\cite{tuba-mouse}: the rat cranial
cavity is anatomically simpler than the macaque (no articulated
mandible, no temporal fossa to seal) and an order of magnitude
larger than the mouse (${\sim}2$\,mL vs.\ ${\sim}0.45$\,mL). In
practice this approach \emph{fails on rat} for the same reason it
fails on macaque: the orbits are large bilateral openings in the dry
cranium (${\sim}6$\,mm diameter each), the foramen magnum is
${\sim}3$\,mm, and the multiple foramina (optic, sphenopalatine, ear
bullae apertures) collectively leak the region-grow boundary in
through every direction. At both the 240\,$\mu$m and 118\,$\mu$m
caches the production cavity from this recipe is the entire
bounding box ($\sim$43\,mL) -- a definite cavity-leak failure mode.
Larger closing radii (up to 6\,mm) seal the orbital walls in the
bone-shell sense but include the orbital pseudocavities in the
``inside the skull'' volume (5.2\,mL instead of $\sim$2\,mL).

We adopt the macaque \textbf{bone-shell cavity}
extractor~\cite{tuba-macaque}, refactored in this work into the
species-agnostic \texttt{tuba.\allowbreak core.\allowbreak cavity.\allowbreak
bone\_shell\_cavity} with all species-specific constants exposed as
keyword arguments, plus two rat-specific extensions developed for
the 118\,$\mu$m voxel pitch: (a) median-smoothing of the per-row /
per-column plug boundary positions, and (b) a virtual ``bowl-shaped
bone dam'' added at the foramen magnum. The production chain has ten
steps:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
  \item \textbf{Bone-shell seed}: bone hysteresis +
        \texttt{SEED\_CLOSE\_MM}\,$=2$\,mm closing + per-column
        curved basicranium floor + per-row curved rostral / caudal
        plugs + per-axial-slice convex hull of closed-bone
        (\texttt{HULL\_X\_HALF\_MM}\,$=12$\,mm; z-dependent rostral
        cutoff $+6$\,/$+10$\,mm at low / high $z$). The four plug
        boundary surfaces are \textbf{median-smoothed with a 2.5\,mm
        kernel} (\texttt{PLUG\_SMOOTH\_MM}\,$=2.5$, exposed as the
        new \texttt{smooth\_plug\_mm} kwarg) so the bone-shell
        contour follows a curved average bone extent rather than
        the jagged per-row / per-column stepped boundary that is
        visible at the rat's high voxel pitch. The macaque pillar's
        250\,$\mu$m voxel pitch hides the stepping via partial
        voluming and uses \texttt{smooth\_plug\_mm}\,$=0$ (default).
        Region-grow ``outside-of-skull'' from the volume boundary;
        cavity = $\lnot$ shell $\land\,\lnot$ outside; largest CC.
        For TMM M-2272 this yields a 1.5\,mL bone-shell seed centred
        on the brain box.
  \item \textbf{ANTs Affine} register the bone-shell seed (moving,
        binary) to the WHS brain mask (fixed, binary, derived from
        the v4.01 annotation as ``any label $>0$''; the T2* template
        is unsuitable as a mask because thresholding at 5\,\% of its
        max yields 7\,mL instead of the true 2.46\,mL brain volume).
        Mean-squares metric, 12 DOF.
  \item \textbf{Inverse-warp} the WHS brain mask back into rat skull
        space with linear interpolation; threshold at 0.5; 2\,mm
        morphological closing for linear-interpolation hygiene.
  \item \textbf{Concave-bowl bone dam at the foramen magnum}: an
        ellipsoidal shell (outer ellipsoid minus inner ellipsoid
        offset rostrally) tilted $+15^\circ$ in the DV/AP plane,
        added to the bone mask. The outer ellipsoid is centred at
        $(0, -22.1, +0.45)$\,mm with radii $(4.5, 2.4, 3.05)$\,mm
        (LR, AP, DV); the inner ellipsoid is centred at
        $(0, -20.7, +0.45)$\,mm with radii $(3.5, 3.0, 2.5)$\,mm;
        the bowl opens rostrally (toward the brain) so the cavity
        is bounded by the bowl's curved concave inner surface,
        mimicking the actual rat occipital bone's curvature around
        the cerebellum. Equivalent bone-mass contribution: 0.045\,mL.
        Coordinates pinned anatomically from the midline-sagittal
        TMM M-2272 view (foramen magnum AP range $-23.5$ to
        $-20.7$\,mm, DV range $-1.6$ to $+2.5$\,mm).
  \item \textbf{Bone-clip} the warped cavity against
        \texttt{(arr > BONE\_LOW) | dam}.
  \item \textbf{Largest-CC} to drop bone-clip-induced islands.
  \item \textbf{1.5\,mm opening} (erode + largest-CC + dilate) to
        disconnect thin fingers extending into basicranium
        sub-cavities (sphenoid foramina, optic canal, ear bullae)
        that are connected to the main cavity through narrow necks.
  \item \textbf{fill\_holes} + repeated bone-clip against bone + dam.
  \item \textbf{4\,mm caudal pad}: shift the cleaned cavity along
        $+j$ storage ($-y$ world = caudal) to extend it to the actual
        most-caudal occipital bone (the bone-shell + Affine produces
        a $\sim$2\,mm caudal undershoot); re-clip against bone + dam.
  \item \textbf{Final largest-CC}.
\end{enumerate}

The production cavity is 2.39\,mL, in the published 1.8--2.4\,mL
adult-rat brain-parenchyma range~\cite{rat-volume}, reaching
$y=-23.15$\,mm (exactly the most-caudal occipital bone) and bounded
by the bowl dam's curved inner surface at the foramen magnum. The
Affine matrix is saved at
\texttt{rat\_cavity\_to\_whs\_affine.mat} under
\texttt{\$TUBA\_RAT\_REG\_DIR}; the same matrix is reused in
\S\ref{sec:registration} for atlas-product warps (no re-fit at the
slab-loader resolution).

\section{WHS atlas warp into rat space}
\label{sec:registration}

The same ANTs Affine that produced the cavity in \S\ref{sec:cavity}
is reused to warp the WHS v1.01 $T_2^*$ template and the v4.01 label
volume into rat space. The Affine is computed once between the rat
seed and the WHS brain mask at 118\,$\mu$m and is
resolution-independent: the inverse warp applies at the 118\,$\mu$m
working cache for visualisation, and could equally apply at the
native 29.6\,$\mu$m resolution for high-frequency acoustic-property
mapping (\S\ref{sec:acoustic-mapping}) without re-fitting. The
$T_2^*$ template is warped with linear interpolation, the integer
label volume with \texttt{nearestNeighbor} interpolation. Wall time
for the Affine fit on 8 cores is $\sim$2\,min at 118\,$\mu$m.

Warped products land at
\texttt{whs\_template\_in\_rat.nii.gz} and
\texttt{whs\_annotation\_in\_rat.nii.gz} in
\texttt{\$TUBA\_RAT\_REG\_DIR}. The warped annotation retains 225
distinct WHS label IDs (the full v4.01 set surviving the
nearest-neighbour resample at this resolution). A spot-check on
three TUS-relevant targets:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
  \item \emph{Caudate putamen} (WHS ID 197): 57\,218 warped voxels =
        0.095\,mL, centroid at $(+0.45, -7.66, +5.98)$\,mm in rat
        aligned-RAS world.
  \item \emph{Corpus callosum and associated subcortical white
        matter} (WHS ID 67): 46\,043 voxels = 0.076\,mL, centroid
        $(+0.37, -9.58, +7.88)$\,mm.
  \item \emph{Frontal association cortex} (WHS ID 77): 6\,999
        voxels = 0.012\,mL, centroid $(+0.33, -2.70, +9.58)$\,mm.
\end{itemize}
All three centroids fall inside the production cavity
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cavity-qc}). The AP ordering matches expected
anatomy: frontal association cortex most rostral ($y=-2.7$\,mm),
caudate putamen mid-brain ($y=-7.7$), corpus callosum mid-brain /
slightly caudal ($y=-9.6$), with the warped cerebellum centroid at
$y=-18.1$\,mm bounding the caudal extent. The 15\,mm rostro-caudal
spread matches the published adult rat brain length.

\section{Acoustic-property mapping}
\label{sec:acoustic-mapping}

We adopt the Maga-style piecewise-linear intensity-to-$(c, \rho)$
ramp from the mouse pipeline~\cite{tuba-mouse} \S8 and the macaque
pipeline~\cite{tuba-macaque} \S Acoustic-property, implemented in
\texttt{tuba.core.slab.\allowbreak IntensityToAcousticRamp}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
  \item $I_{\mathrm{low}}$: voxels at or below map to water /
        soft-tissue ($c = 1540$\,m/s, $\rho = 1000$\,kg/m$^3$).
  \item $I_{\mathrm{high}}$: voxels at or above saturate at
        cortical-bone values ($c = 2900$\,m/s,
        $\rho = 1900$\,kg/m$^3$).
  \item Linear interpolation in between.
\end{itemize}
The endpoints in \texttt{tuba.species.rat.SLAB\_RAMP} are anchored
on the histogram of \S\ref{sec:orientation}: $I_{\mathrm{low}}=50$
(air/soft-tissue separator), $I_{\mathrm{high}}=170$ (lower edge of
the cortical-bone tail; the tail extends to $\sim$245 but the bulk
sits around 170--210). The endocranial cavity from \S\ref{sec:cavity} contains air
in the source scan and is overwritten with brain-parenchymal
acoustic properties for the simulation ($c = 1540$\,m/s,
$\rho = 1040$\,kg/m$^3$); cavity voxels classified as bone (e.g.\
falx cerebri or sutural protrusions surviving the extractor) keep
their intensity-derived values.

\paragraph{Attenuation.}
We use linear-frequency-dependent attenuation with
$\alpha_{\mathrm{bone}} = 8$\,dB/cm/MHz and
$\alpha_{\mathrm{tissue}} = 0.5$\,dB/cm/MHz, consistent with the
mouse and macaque pipelines. At 500\,kHz this gives 4\,dB/cm bone
and 0.25\,dB/cm tissue. Validation against transmission measurements
through the TMM M-2272 cranium is not possible (the specimen is not
physically available); the pipeline therefore reports relative
focal-pressure ratios rather than absolute transmission, as in the
mouse Maga validation~\cite{tuba-mouse} \S Focal comparison.

\section{Bowl placement and forward simulation}
\label{sec:placement}

The placement helper \texttt{tuba.species.\allowbreak rat.\allowbreak
place\_on\_skull} computes the apex / target / beam triple for a
single-element bowl aimed at a WHS-defined brain target. It accepts a
canonical WHS structure name (resolved via
\texttt{tuba.\allowbreak atlases.\allowbreak whs.\allowbreak
WaxholmSpace.\allowbreak structure\_id}, which parses the
\texttt{.label} table at first call), reads the centroid of that
structure in the warped annotation volume in rat
aligned-native world-mm, builds the outer-bone surface from the
118\,$\mu$m cache via close+fill, and picks the highest-$z$ surface
voxel within \texttt{lateral\_search\_mm}\,$=3$\,mm of the target
$(x, y)$ as the apex candidate. The apex is then lifted along the
local outward normal by the amount required to make the
apex-to-target distance equal to the geometric focal length supplied
by the caller (\texttt{apex\_to\_target\_mm}).

Default focal-length / bowl geometry tracks the rat-tFUS
literature~\cite{rat-cc-tFUS,rat-stim-strialum}: $f_0 = 500$\,kHz,
$D = 20$\,mm, $\mathrm{ROC} = 20$\,mm (f/1). At this frequency the
parenchymal wavelength is 3.08\,mm, so the 118\,$\mu$m slab cache
samples $\sim$26\,vox/$\lambda$ in soft tissue and $\sim$49\,vox/$\lambda$
in cortical bone -- well above the 6--10 vox/$\lambda$ threshold for
angular-spectrum convergence. Apex selection inside
\texttt{place\_on\_skull} follows the mouse recipe: highest-$z$
outer-bone voxel within \texttt{lateral\_search\_mm}\,$=3$\,mm of
the target $(x, y)$ position, lifted along $+z$ to make the
apex-to-target Euclidean distance equal to the geometric focal
length.

\paragraph{Slab loader.}
\texttt{tuba.species.rat.\allowbreak load\_slab} returns
$(c, \rho, dz_{\mathrm{slab}}, \mathrm{frame})$ with the same
signature as \texttt{tuba.species.\allowbreak mouse.\allowbreak
load\_slab} and \texttt{tuba.species.\allowbreak macaque.\allowbreak
load\_slab}, so any FDTD or angular-spectrum driver wired against
one species runs on the others unchanged. The default cache is the
118\,$\mu$m factor-4 block-max NIfTI; a 237\,$\mu$m cache override is
available for low-frequency exploratory runs.

\section{Lessons carried in from the other TUBA pillars}
\label{sec:lessons}

The rat pillar is the fourth species in TUBA; the first three pillars
(mouse, macaque, human) yielded six recurring lessons that we expect
to apply unchanged:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Work at atlas-matching resolution for the cavity contour.}
      The mouse pipeline~\cite{tuba-mouse} showed visible staircase
      artefacts at the 200\,$\mu$m ANTs cache and a ${\sim}0.4$\,mm
      inward boundary shift from block-max smearing; moving to the
      Allen 25\,$\mu$m grid removed both. For rat the corresponding
      cache is 118\,$\mu$m (vs.\ the WHS 39\,$\mu$m grid) --- closer
      than mouse, looser than macaque.
\item \textbf{Stop the cavity at the bone-low inner surface, not
      bone-high.} Extending the cavity to the cortical bone-high
      inner edge leaks through sutures and thin spots
      (mouse~\cite{tuba-mouse}); the bone-low surface is ${\sim}1$
      cortical-bone thickness inside the true inner surface ---
      topologically reliable, geometrically near.
\item \textbf{Don't deform a smooth atlas mask to fit a noisy
      cavity.} The macaque pipeline~\cite{tuba-macaque} rejected an
      SyN fit between bone-shell and NMT brain mask precisely because
      SyN deforms the warped mask to track cavity-side irregularity;
      Affine preserves atlas topology. For rat we use the
      macaque Affine recipe from the start; the rat's many anatomical
      openings (orbits, foramen magnum) preclude the mouse-style
      hysteresis approach.
\item \textbf{Bone-as-constraint, not bone-as-target.} After warping,
      clip the cavity against the species-specific bone mask
      (\texttt{cavity \& \textasciitilde bone\_mask}). Sharpens the
      inner cortical-table boundary without reintroducing CC
      fragmentation~\cite{tuba-macaque}.
\item \textbf{Coordinate-frame discipline.} Slab loader and placement
      helper both expect apex and beam coordinates in
      world-mm in the aligned-native frame, not voxel-mm. ANTs forward
      transforms saved from
      \texttt{ants.registration(fixed=cavity, moving=atlas)} warp
      atlas $\to$ skull via \texttt{apply\_transforms}, the inverse
      for the other direction. The rat module follows the mouse
      convention exactly.
\item \textbf{Drive calibration is post-hoc and species-agnostic.}
      Free-field MI target at $p_0 = 1$\,Pa with post-hoc rescaling
      lets the relative skull-transmission factor drop out of the
      unit-drive focal-pressure ratio cleanly --- preserved unchanged
      across all four TUBA pillars.
\end{itemize}

\section{Calibration trail and the choices that fell out of it}
\label{sec:lessons-rat}

Three rat-specific lessons emerged during this pillar's calibration
that did not appear in the mouse, macaque, or human reports. They
are documented here so the next species port (marmoset?\ ferret?)
can avoid the same dead ends.

\paragraph{1. Storage convention is observable from the dental
arcade.} The TMM M-2272 storage convention
$(\mathrm{slice}{=}AP, \mathrm{row}{=}DV, \mathrm{col}{=}LR)$ is the
\emph{macaque} convention, not the mouse one. We resolved this
visually from anchor slice 568, where the dental palate forms an
inverted-U at the bottom of the image. The
\texttt{swap\_row\_col=True} kwarg added to
\texttt{tuba.core.frame.apply\_axis\_flips} in this work is the
mechanism by which the macaque/rat convention threads into the
mouse-shaped pipeline without changing downstream code. The mouse
pillar~\cite{tuba-mouse} keeps \texttt{swap\_row\_col=False}; the
new flag is backwards-compatible.

\paragraph{2. \texttt{find\_pca\_rotation}'s eigenvalue-to-axis
assignment is mouse-specific.} On a dry rat skull (no mandible)
with \texttt{ALIGN\_PCA\_THRESH}\,$=120$ the variance ordering is
AP\,$>$\,DV\,$>$\,LR, not AP\,$>$\,LR\,$>$\,DV. The function's
hard-coded ``second-largest is LR'' rule produces a 85$^\circ$
spurious $R_y$ rotation. Since the rat skull is already
axis-aligned after the row/col swap, we set the pre-alignment
angles to zero and document the failure mode rather than fixing the
function -- a generic fix would require either a heuristic that
re-orders the middle and smallest eigenvectors by alignment with
the world axes, or a separate species-specific permutation. The
\texttt{find\_pca\_rotation} mouse + macaque cases continue to work
unchanged.

\paragraph{3. Pure-hysteresis cavity extractor fails on rat for the
same reasons it fails on macaque.} The orbits (${\sim}6$\,mm
diameter) and foramen magnum (${\sim}3$\,mm) are wider than any
practical morphological closing radius can bridge at 118\,$\mu$m,
and the region-grow boundary in
\texttt{tuba.core.cavity.extract\_cavity} (the mouse recipe) leaks
through every remaining aperture and consumes the entire bounding
box (43\,mL). An attempted heavy-closing-plus-fill\_holes seed (6\,mm
closing, 3D fill) was the wrong shape -- box-shaped, not
brain-shaped -- and the subsequent Affine fit to the WHS brain mask
either over-scaled (3.6\,mL) or mis-located the warp into the
ventral pseudo-cavity. \textbf{The macaque bone-shell extractor},
already in the codebase at
\texttt{tuba.\_macaque.\allowbreak fast\_iter\_250um}, solves both
problems: per-column curved basicranium floor + per-row curved
plugs + per-axial-slice convex hull with z-dependent rostral cutoff
gives an anatomically-tight seed (1.34\,mL bone-shell cavity). The
subsequent Affine to the WHS brain mask scales it back up to the
correct 2.39\,mL brain shape. In this work we refactored the
macaque function into the species-agnostic
\texttt{tuba.core.cavity.\allowbreak bone\_shell\_cavity} with all
species-specific constants exposed as keyword arguments, so the
rat species module and the macaque pipeline can share the same
implementation. The mouse pillar continues to use the simpler
\texttt{extract\_cavity} (small foramina; bone-shell builder is
unnecessary overhead).

\paragraph{4. The bone-shell builder produces a stepped boundary
at high voxel pitch; smooth the plug positions and add a virtual
bowl dam at the foramen magnum.} The macaque
\texttt{bone\_shell\_cavity} builder constructs the bone-bounded
interior from a per-column basicranium floor and per-row rostral /
caudal plugs. At the macaque's 250\,$\mu$m voxel pitch the
per-column / per-row stepping is hidden by partial voluming and
produces a smooth visual contour; at the rat's 118\,$\mu$m the
stepping is resolved and shows up as a jagged cavity boundary
(visible artifact in QC plots even when the cavity volume is
correct). We add a new \texttt{smooth\_plug\_mm} kwarg to
\texttt{bone\_shell\_cavity} that median-smooths the per-row /
per-column boundary positions in 2D before the plug masks are
built; \texttt{PLUG\_SMOOTH\_MM}\,$=2.5$ for rat,
\texttt{0} (default, unchanged) for macaque. Separately, the
foramen-magnum opening leaks the Affine-warped WHS brain mask past
the occipital bone. We add a virtual \textbf{concave-bowl bone dam}
(outer ellipsoid minus inner ellipsoid offset rostrally, tilted
$+15^\circ$ in the DV/AP plane) at the anatomically pinned foramen
magnum location ($y\in[-23.5, -20.7]$, $z\in[-1.6, +2.5]$\,mm).
The bowl's concave side faces rostrally so the cavity is bounded
by the bowl's curved inner surface, mimicking the actual occipital
bone's curvature. The dam contributes 0.045\,mL of virtual bone to
the bone-clip mask but never enters the cavity itself. With
smoothed plugs + bowl dam + the 1.5\,mm opening + 4\,mm caudal
pad, the cavity is 2.39\,mL with a smooth contour, contained
inside the bone walls in all directions.

\paragraph{5. Bone-shell hull constants are expressed relative to a
bone-centroid-at-origin affine.} The macaque hull parameters
(\texttt{Y\_HULL\_MAX\_MM\_LOWZ}\,=\,$-3$\,mm, etc.) sit in the
macaque world frame after the PCA-driven
\texttt{align\_native\_to\_axes} step, which both rotates the volume
and translates so the bone centroid lands at world origin
$(0,0,0)$. The rat pipeline skips the rotation (\S\ref{sec:align})
but initially also skipped the translation, leaving the bone
centroid offset from world origin by ${\sim}(+0.3, -0.9, +1.2)$\,mm
(the bone centroid is biased rostrally because the dental palate is
the densest part of the rat skull). With the macaque hull constants
plugged in unchanged, the per-axial hull's rostral cutoff at
$y\le-3$\,mm world ended up at $y\le-3+0.9=-2.1$\,mm
``bone-centroid relative'' --- still rostral enough to capture the
brain box but also pulling the cavity centroid 10--15\,mm too
rostral, into the orbital pseudo-cavities. The fix is to compute
the bone centroid in the current world frame and pass a
\emph{centred} affine to
\texttt{bone\_shell\_cavity}: a 4-line patch in
\texttt{extract\_cavity}, after which the macaque constants apply
directly. With this fix the production cavity centroid lands at
$y=-11.3$\,mm and the warped structure centroids fall in the
expected AP order (frontal $-2.7$, caudate $-7.8$, CC $-9.6$,
cerebellum $-18.1$).

\paragraph{6. Brain mask must come from the atlas annotation, not a
threshold of the intensity template.} A naive threshold of the WHS
$T_2^*$ template at 5\,\% of its max gives a 7\,mL ``brain mask''
because the template carries substantial soft-tissue and air
background. Thresholds 1--30\,\% all give 4--15\,mL; none recover
the true 2.5\,mL brain volume. The v4.01 annotation
(\S\ref{sec:whs}) encodes the curated brain extent as
``any label $>0$'' and produces a 2.458\,mL mask -- the correct
volume. The \texttt{tuba.atlases.\allowbreak whs.\allowbreak
WaxholmSpace.\allowbreak derive\_brain\_mask} method now uses the
annotation; the Allen CCFv3 binding for the mouse already works
this way (label-volume-derived mask).

\section{Open items}
\label{sec:open}

The pipeline is end-to-end functional and the rat pillar is ready
to drive transcranial-FUS simulation experiments. Remaining items
are quality improvements rather than blockers:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
  \item Build a native-resolution (29.6\,$\mu$m) cache + cavity
        upsample for high-frequency simulations beyond
        500\,kHz. The macaque manuscript~\cite{tuba-macaque} \S
        Native cavity describes the resample recipe.
  \item Run a forward simulation through the slab loader and produce
        a focal-prediction figure analogous to the mouse
        manuscript~\cite{tuba-mouse} \S Focal comparison. The
        slab loader plumbing is wired but no specific bowl-target
        choice has been published in the literature for the TMM
        M-2272 specimen; this report leaves the focal numbers to
        the calling experiment.
  \item Add a smoke test that exercises the Affine-warp recipe (not
        just the manifest+atlas parsing currently covered in
        \texttt{tests/test\_rat\_skeleton.py}). The test would need
        the WHS atlas + a tiny mock rat-skull NIfTI to keep CI
        runtime under a second.
\end{enumerate}
